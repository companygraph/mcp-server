# A use case is drawn in its scope, as its scenario and by what it is held to — design

> Asked about a use case, the chat answers in words and a table: the requirements pack holds who has the goal, what the use case includes and asks of which features, the steps and where each goes another way, and the requirements a feature, a product or a use case is held to, and no shape of `diagram` reads any of it as what it is. Requirements engineering draws these in three pictures: the use case in its scope with its actor, as UML draws it; the scenario as a flow with its extensions, as Cockburn writes it; and the requirements of a thing, grouped by kind. This design adds the three as shapes of `diagram`, the first and the third shown together when a visitor asks to see a use case and the second when they ask how it runs, with one new mark in the widget for an actor who is not one of the company's own.

Status: proposed. Decided on 2026-10-12 with the owner against this repository at `3d10caf` (v0.67.0), `companygraph/chat-server` at `3bff2cc` (v0.34.0), `robertblust/design` at `6c704a8` (v0.150.0), `companygraph/meta-model` at `4088f5a` (v0.94.0, core 0.69.0), whose requirements pack declares the types read here, and `companygraph/mental-model` at `26ad32b`, which took the pack with fifteen use cases and thirty-two requirements. The owner chose from a mockup drawn with the widget's own Mermaid and settings, in companygraph.io's page shell, from those pages: he liked all three pictures; chose the outlined figure for an actor nobody in the company holds, "someone, not one of us"; and chose that the scenario is drawn alone, when asked for, as the software pack's flow is, while the use case and its requirements are drawn together, as a system and what it holds are.

---

## 1. The gap

The requirements pack gives each picture what it draws. A use case names its `primary-actor` as a seat where the company has one, its `level` and `scope`, the `features` it asks something of and the use cases its steps call in `includes`; its `## Stakeholders and interests`, `## Main success scenario` and `## Extensions` carry the steps with who acts and each condition that sends a step another way; a requirement carries its `kind` and `modality`, the rules it `follows`, and an `## Applies to` table naming the features, products and use cases it is about.

No shape reads them as what they are. The neighborhood of a use case draws every edge one hop out, labeled by the field that drew it, `includes`, `features`, `Applies to.Entity`, with the actor, the level, the steps and the modality nowhere on the picture, and the chat on companygraph.io answers "show me the use case Ask the company a question" with a table.

**What the change buys is a use case drawn the way the practice draws it, in its scope, as its scenario and by what it is held to, every node an entity and every arrow an edge the model draws, for any client of an MCP host and not only the chat, with companygraph.io the first site to show its own.**

## 2. The three shapes

`diagram`'s `shape` gains three values, `usecase`, `scenario` and `requirements`.

**`usecase`** takes the `id` of a use case and draws it in its scope, a `flowchart LR`. The use case is the middle, a rounded box, `(["…"])`, its name in bold with its `level` in `<small>` as a stereotype, `«user goal»`, and the class `middle`. Where the page writes `scope`, the middle stands in a frame titled with the token, `system`; where it does not, there is no frame. The primary actor stands outside the frame, a box with a mark before the name, joined to the middle by a solid arrow; its mark is the human's where a human holds the seat, the agent's where only agents hold it, and the new actor's where nobody does or where the actor is a string the page keeps, as the schema allows, a party outside the company. A string actor is a box in the source and in no `nodes`, since it is no entity, as the neighborhood's `more` box is not. Each use case `includes` names stands in the frame as a rounded box with its own level, reached from the middle by a dashed arrow labeled `includes`. Each feature `features` names stands to the right as a box with `«feature»`, reached by a solid arrow labeled `features`. Each requirement whose `## Applies to` names the use case stands as a hexagon, `{{"…"}}`, its name in bold and `«kind · modality»` below, joined to the middle by a dashed arrow with no label; its class is `must` where its modality is `must`, a heavier border, and `may` where it is `may`, a dashed one, and the source defines each class it uses.

**`scenario`** takes the same `id` and draws the use case's main success scenario, a `flowchart TB`. Where the page writes `## Trigger`, its text is the first node, a rounded box, in no `nodes`, since a trigger is prose; then one box per row of `## Main success scenario`, in the table's order, each joined to the next by a solid arrow: the actor's mark, the actor's name in bold, the step's label in `<small>` and the action. A step's box is a node naming the seat its actor resolves to, so a click opens the seat; a step whose actor is a string is a box in no `nodes`. Each row of `## Extensions` is a box with the class `ext`, dashed, holding the step's label in `<small>`, the condition, and the handling in italics, reached from the step it branches from by a dashed arrow; a label that names no step of the scenario is drawn from nowhere and reported in `omitted`. The answer carries what the lifecycle's `transitions` carry for states: `steps`, one per row, `step`, `actor` and `action`, and `extensions`, one per row, `step`, `condition` and `handling`, so a client states the scenario without reading the picture.

**`requirements`** takes the `id` of a feature, a product, a use case or a requirement, and draws by the id's type, as `aggregate` draws a context or an aggregate. Given a feature, a product or a use case, it is the thing in the middle with its type as stereotype and the class `middle`, and every requirement whose `## Applies to` names it, one hexagon each in a frame per kind present, in the order `functional`, `quality`, `constraint`, each joined to the middle by a solid arrow with no label, and each rule a requirement `follows` as a box with `«rule»`, reached by a dotted arrow labeled `follows`. Given a requirement, it is the hexagon in the middle with `«kind · modality»`, each entity its `## Applies to` names as a box, or a rounded box for a use case, with its type as stereotype, reached by a solid arrow labeled `Applies to`, and each rule it follows by a dotted arrow labeled `follows`. The modality classes are the use case picture's.

`title` is the use case's name for the first two and the middle's name for the third. `nodes` names every box and hexagon with its entity, typed `use-case`, `seat`, `feature`, `product`, `requirement` or `rule`. `links` carries one entry per arrow with the model's word: `includes`, `features`, `Applies to`, `follows`, and for a scenario's arrows the step label on the arrow's far end, `2`, or the extension's, `2a`. `edges` counts them.

**Refusals.** Past fifty nodes a shape refuses with `cannot_draw` and `too_large`. `usecase` always holds its middle and is never refused as empty. `scenario` of a use case whose `## Main success scenario` has no row refuses with `cannot_draw` and `empty`. `requirements` of a feature, a product or a use case that no requirement applies to refuses with `cannot_draw` and `empty`; of a requirement, it always holds its middle. An id of another type is `invalid_argument` on `id`, naming the types the shape takes. A model that does not take the requirements pack has no `use-case` type, and every shape refuses with `unknown_type`, as every shape refuses a type its model lacks; `requirements` given a feature of such a model refuses the same way, since the type it would draw is missing.

**Escaping.** `label()` is unchanged. A step's action and an extension's condition and handling are prose and may hold anything; they go through `label()` as a title does, and the three mark tokens the shapes write, `fak:fa-human`, `fak:fa-agent` and `fak:fa-actor`, are written outside it.

## 3. The widget

`robertblust/design`'s `chat.js` registers a seventh mark in the icon pack `fak`, `actor`: the figure outlined, the circle and the shoulders in `currentColor` with no fill, "someone, not one of us", defined in `lib/marks.mjs` beside the others and held to the widget's copy by the existing test, and colored at the firm brightness as a person is. `chat.css` gives a node of the class `ext` no fill, as it gives an open position none, so an extension reads as a branch and not as a step; the hexagon, the rounded box, the dashes and the heavier borders are the source's own.

The captions gain the three shapes: "Use case", "Scenario" and "Requirements". Under each picture the widget writes its reading line, from its own strings and never from the host. For `usecase`: "The actor stands outside the frame, which is the use case's scope; a dashed arrow runs from a use case to one its steps include, a solid one to a feature it asks something of; a hexagon is a requirement it is held to, with a heavy border where it must and a dashed one where it may." For `scenario`: "The rounded box is the trigger; each box is a step, in order, with who acts; a dashed box is an extension, the condition first and what happens then." For `requirements`: "A hexagon is a requirement, with a heavy border where it must and a dashed one where it may, in a frame for its kind; an arrow runs to what it applies to, a dotted one to a rule it follows." Their German is made by the translator from the reviewed English, read by the editor and the back-reader, and settled by the owner.

## 4. The chat

`companygraph/chat-server`'s `DIAGRAM_RULE` in `lib/prompt.mjs` gains the three shapes and two clauses: a visitor who asks to see a use case, or the diagram of one, who wants what from it or what it is held to, is shown shape `usecase` and then shape `requirements`, both with the use case's id, and the answer names what each drew; where `requirements` refuses as `empty`, the use case stands alone and the answer says in one sentence that the model names no requirement it is held to. One who asks how a use case runs, its steps, or what happens when something goes wrong, is shown shape `scenario` alone, with its id. One who asks what a feature or a product is held to is shown shape `requirements` with that id, and one who asks about a requirement by its name is shown shape `requirements` with the requirement's id. The note in `lib/loop.mjs` words each line from the use case's side, `includes`, `features`, a requirement `applies to` what it names and `follows` a rule, and a scenario's arrow as one step leading to the next or to its extension, so the model does not read a line backwards, as the measurement for the system's pictures showed it would.

## 5. CompanyGraph's own model

`companygraph/mental-model` holds what the shapes draw since it took the pack at `26ad32b`: fifteen use cases, Keep the company's model including ten of them, and thirty-two requirements, twenty-six applying to a feature, three to a product, three to a use case, and three following a rule. Nothing is added to it by this design. companygraph.io shows the pictures once its pins reach that commit, which the talk on the pack moves, and its host once it takes this release.

## 6. The interface

`shape` takes three new values, which INTERFACE.md counts as additive, in the input enum, the output's and `cannot_draw`'s details. The three join `TAKES.id` and require it. `scenario`'s answer carries `steps` and `extensions`, two lists new to that shape alone, as `lifecycle` carries `transitions` and `schema` carries `everyType`; the output schema declares them optional. Nodes typed `use-case`, `requirement` and `rule` are new and use the fields every node has. The tool's description in `lib/tools.mjs` names the shapes within the length the template allows, and the `id` argument's description names what `id` is for each.

## 9. Release and order

As the system's pictures went. The tool first: an `mcp-server` release, the next minor, and the three hosts re-pinned to it. Then `design`'s widget, released and taken by the three sites, which draws the actor's mark for any host that sends it. Then `chat-server`'s clauses, released and moved into each deployment after its site has taken the design release. Every merge, every release and every re-pin waits for the owner's word. blust.ch and guestgraph.io see no change: neither model takes the requirements pack, and the three shapes refuse there with `unknown_type`.

## 10. What the mockup showed

The mockup ran companygraph.io's page shell with the design package's `chat.js`, `chat.css` and vendored Mermaid at v0.147.0, the actor's mark registered by the page as §3 registers it, and figures built from CompanyGraph's own pages at `26ad32b`. It confirmed what this design takes: the use case in its scope reads at a glance, and the summary use case with ten includes draws as a map of what the owner does with a model; the scenario of a use case with eight steps and ten extensions is tall, which the fitted preview and Expand carry, and is why it is drawn when asked for; the requirements of a feature read as the product owner's list, a frame per kind; and a single requirement is small, which is why it shares a shape with the requirements of a thing rather than having one of its own.

## 11. Out of scope

A stakeholder picture, who has an interest in a use case and what each needs, which is a table a visitor reads in words. A map of every use case of a model, which the summary use case's picture gives where a company writes one. The requirements of a whole model by kind. A trace from a use case through a feature to the software pack's feature design, which crosses two packs and is a shape for a later design. These shapes on the model's own pages or in the Obsidian plugin.

## Decisions

Taken by the owner on 2026-10-12 from the mockup: all three pictures; the outlined figure for an actor nobody in the company holds, "someone, not one of us"; the use case and its requirements drawn together, the scenario alone when asked how it runs. One shape for the requirements of a thing and for a single requirement, by the id's type, is the Specifier's, as are the captions, "Use case", "Scenario" and "Requirements", for the owner's review on the branch.
